# IML Country Profile Scoring Protocol

## Purpose

IML Country Profile scores describe the documented capacity of a health-information environment. They are evidence profiles, not league tables. A country must not receive credit merely because a policy, platform or contact channel formally exists: implementation, correction, answerability and institutional follow-through also matter.

The framework retains six domains:

1. Governance and standards;
2. Technical interoperability;
3. Identity and trust;
4. Adoption and clinical use;
5. Security and resilience;
6. Feedback, correction and learning.

## Feedback, correction and learning indicators

- **LRN-1 — Correction mechanisms:** mechanisms for correcting erroneous information across systems.
- **LRN-2 — Continuous improvement:** use of interoperability and outcome indicators to support learning.
- **LRN-3 — Traceable correction and reuse:** correction, versioning and traceable reuse of database-held information.
- **LRN-4 — Institutional documentary reliability and integrity:** accessibility, stability, precision, attribution, maintenance and coherence of institutional evidence.
- **LRN-5 — Institutional responsiveness and answerability:** the demonstrated capacity of responsible institutions to receive, examine and substantively answer relevant documented inquiries, and to act on authoritative findings, warnings and recommendations.

LRN-5 belongs inside the Learning domain. It does not create a seventh domain and must not replace LRN-3 or LRN-4.

## LRN-5 assessment scale

| Score | Observable performance |
|---:|---|
| 100 | Substantive response or corrective action within 30 days, with traceable follow-through. |
| 75 | Substantive response or corrective action within 31–60 days. |
| 50 | Delayed or incomplete response, but with usable information or partial corrective action. |
| 25 | Acknowledgement, generic reply or formal process without a substantive answer or effective follow-through. |
| 0 | Persistent non-response, non-enforcement or failure to act on documented problems after a reasonable opportunity to respond or correct them. |
| Not assessed | Evidence is insufficient, the inquiry is outside the institution's remit, or receipt cannot be established. |

An automated acknowledgement is not a substantive institutional response.

## Passive institutional obstruction

Persistent institutional silence or inaction is not neutral. It prevents scrutiny, clarification, correction and learning. IML records its functional effect as **passive institutional obstruction** without claiming to establish the private intention of individual officials.

The following can support a zero or very low LRN-5 assessment:

- no substantive answer for more than 180 days after a relevant documented inquiry and at least two follow-ups;
- repeated failure to implement or answer authoritative audit findings;
- unresolved institutional blockages documented across successive reviews;
- nominal correction or recourse channels that do not produce usable decisions or corrections;
- persistent failure to evaluate a programme before changing its objectives or continuing its deployment.

## Evidence rules

Authoritative reports from courts of audit, parliamentary bodies, regulators, ombuds institutions or equivalent independent control bodies may directly reduce LRN-5 when they document repeated, unresolved failures of evaluation, enforcement, correction or institutional follow-through. Such evidence is not an individual anecdote and does not require publication of personal correspondence.

Individual correspondence may corroborate the assessment when the institution, remit, question, dates, follow-ups and outcome are retained in an auditable record. Personal details should be redacted before publication.

Evidence must state its scope and limitations. A report about selected national programmes does not automatically establish the performance of every institution or every health-information pathway.

## France — decision recorded 1 September 2026

The French Court of Auditors report *Sécurité sociale 2024 — Mon espace santé : des conditions de réussite encore à réunir* documents objectives changed without adequate prior evaluation, uptake below expectations, limited enforceability of obligations and persistent institutional blockages. As an authoritative institutional-control source already included in the France profile, it supports a direct reduction of the Learning domain score.

Pending a broader reassessment of all six domains, the France **Feedback, Correction and Learning** score is revised from **15 to 10**. The Court of Auditors report supports both the existing LRN-3 assessment and the new LRN-5 assessment. Private unanswered correspondence may be retained as corroborating evidence but is not required for this correction.
