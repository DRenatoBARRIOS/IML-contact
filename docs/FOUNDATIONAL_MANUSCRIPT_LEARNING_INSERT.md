## Copy-paste text for Part IV — section 4.9

### Operational Learning indicators

For assessment purposes, the Feedback, Correction and Learning domain is organised around five complementary indicators. **LRN-1 — Correction mechanisms** examines whether erroneous information can be corrected across systems. **LRN-2 — Continuous improvement** examines whether interoperability, quality and outcome indicators are used to support learning. **LRN-3 — Traceable correction and reuse** examines whether corrections, versioning and later reuse of database-held information remain traceable over time. **LRN-4 — Institutional documentary reliability and integrity** examines whether public institutions maintain official evidence that remains accessible, stable, precise, attributable, current and correctable. **LRN-5 — Institutional responsiveness and answerability** examines the demonstrated capacity of responsible institutions to receive, examine and substantively answer relevant documented inquiries, and to act on authoritative findings, warnings and recommendations.

These indicators extend the concept of a learning ecosystem beyond technical error correction. A mature ecosystem should not only detect a problem; it should be able to correct the information concerned, preserve the trace of that correction, maintain trustworthy documentation, respond to documented concerns and demonstrate follow-through. Formal availability alone is therefore insufficient. A policy, platform, reporting channel or contact address should not receive full maturity credit merely because it exists if it does not produce usable correction, answerability or institutional learning in practice.

## Copy-paste text for Part V — methodology

### Institutional responsiveness and answerability

IML treats institutional responsiveness as a measurable component of the Feedback, Correction and Learning domain rather than as a seventh domain. The reference LRN-5 scale distinguishes substantive and traceable follow-through from mere formal availability: **100** indicates a substantive response or corrective action within 30 days with traceable follow-through; **75** indicates substantive action within 31–60 days; **50** indicates a delayed or incomplete response that nevertheless provides usable information or partial corrective action; **25** indicates acknowledgement, a generic reply or a formal process without a substantive answer or effective follow-through; and **0** indicates persistent non-response, non-enforcement or failure to act on documented problems after a reasonable opportunity to respond or correct them. Where evidence is insufficient, the institution is outside the relevant remit, or receipt cannot be established, the indicator should be recorded as **not assessed** rather than scored negatively. An automated acknowledgement is not considered a substantive institutional response.

Persistent institutional silence or inaction is not methodologically neutral when it prevents scrutiny, clarification, correction or learning. IML records this functional effect as **passive institutional obstruction**, without claiming to establish the private intention of individual officials. Evidence may include prolonged non-response after documented follow-up, repeated failure to act on authoritative audit findings, unresolved institutional blockages documented across successive reviews, nominal correction or recourse channels that do not produce usable outcomes, or continued deployment or redirection of programmes without adequate evaluation of known problems.

Authoritative reports from courts of audit, parliamentary bodies, regulators, ombuds institutions or equivalent independent control bodies may directly inform LRN-5 when they document repeated and unresolved failures of evaluation, enforcement, correction or institutional follow-through. Such evidence should not be treated as an individual anecdote. Individual correspondence may be retained as corroborating evidence when the institution, remit, question, dates, follow-ups and outcome are documented, with personal details redacted before publication. Every assessment must state its scope and limitations: a report concerning selected programmes does not automatically establish the performance of every institution or every health-information pathway.

## Copy-paste text for Part VII — section 7.6 France

The French profile also illustrates why a learning ecosystem cannot be assessed only through the existence of formal institutions, strategies or national digital-health services. In the IML country evidence reviewed on 1 September 2026, the French Court of Auditors report *Sécurité sociale 2024 — Mon espace santé : des conditions de réussite encore à réunir* documents objectives changed without adequate prior evaluation, uptake below expectations, limited enforceability of obligations and persistent institutional blockages. Within the IML methodology, this authoritative control evidence supports both traceable concerns about correction and follow-through and the newer LRN-5 indicator on institutional responsiveness and answerability. Pending a broader reassessment of all six domains, the exploratory France score for **Feedback, Correction and Learning** was therefore revised from **15 to 10**. This is not a judgement on France as a whole, nor does the cited report measure every institution or pathway. It is a documented example of the gap that can exist between formal digital-health capacity and the practical ability of an ecosystem to evaluate, correct and learn from known problems.

## Editorial consistency note

`INS-9 Institutional responsiveness to external proposals` may remain in the Institutional Engagement catalogue because it measures whether procedures and contact pathways exist. LRN-5 measures something different: whether responsible institutions actually answer and follow through. The distinction preserves the manuscript's six-domain structure while preventing formal process from being mistaken for operational learning.
